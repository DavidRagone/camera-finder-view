Click a row to load it into the highlighted finder. “Apparent width” is the angle the framed image fills at your eye: the whole screen on an SLR, the frame for the focal length after @ on a rangefinder. ≈ marks an estimate rather than a confirmed spec.

How to read it

  • SLR: you look at a ground-glass screen, so out-of-focus things blur and the image dims when stopped down. A split image shifts sideways until focused; a microprism ring breaks up and shimmers.
  • Rangefinder: you look through a window, so everything is sharp at every distance. Focus by merging the ghost image in the central patch. Bright lines show the lens’s crop; the window does not change with the lens.
  • Size: on an SLR, magnification × coverage sets how big the image looks. On a rangefinder the frame also depends on the lens: a 0.55× finder with a 35 mm frame fills about the same angle as a 0.8× SLR, though everything in it looks further away. True size draws every finder at one angular scale.

What is simulated and what is not

  • Blur, split-image shift and rangefinder ghost offset are computed from focal length, aperture, focus distance and rangefinder base. Their sizes are physical, so use Centre ×5 to see them clearly.
  • Meter readouts follow the light, aperture and shutter sliders (ISO 400). Their position and lettering are schematic, not traced from each camera.
  • On autofocus bodies the focus-confirmation mark lights when anything in the scene sits at the focused distance. The Canon T90, Minolta Maxxum 7000 and Contax G2 still share one generic readout strip.
  • Screen brightness, tint, patch shape and rangefinder window size are approximations. Eye relief, dioptre, flare and dust are not modelled.
